Evidence archive in motion: five shapes

Each comp is a left-to-right strip, not a slide: the path of one search runs across the screen. They differ in what the "zoom" does: move a camera, open the strip, or slide it under a lens.

A · The ribbon one long horizontal page

A single strip as wide as the story, scrolled sideways like a panorama. A minimap on top shows where you are.

  • Zoom: none, really; you scroll past each step.
  • Feels like: a museum timeline wall.

+ simplest; reads like a page; works on a phone. − no "pull back and see it all" moment; weakest motion.

B · The subway line focus + context

The whole path is always on screen as a thin line of stations. The active station opens up, above and below the line, into its detail; the others shrink to dots but stay visible.

  • Zoom: the line squeezes, the station expands in place (an accordion along the track). Zoom out = every station back to a dot.
  • Branch: the evidence bundle hangs below the gate as a spur line; local vs AWS can be two parallel tracks.

+ you never lose the whole path, which is the point of a pipeline; reads at any aspect ratio; the "zoom out, then in" is one motion. − a dense step (the corpus of 12 docs) needs the expanded station to be tall.

C · The camera the first draft, already generated

Stations are panels on a large canvas. A camera pulls back to see the whole canvas, then flies in on the next panel.

  • Zoom: a real camera move (Prezi, or Claude Motion's camera style).
  • File: docs/archive-motion.html, generated from the code.

+ the most cinematic; exports cleanly to MP4. − each zoomed-in view is still a slide-shaped frame, the thing you want to avoid; the path disappears while you're zoomed in.

D · The lens follow the query

The strip slides right to left under a fixed magnifier in the middle of the screen. The query is a glowing token that stays in the lens; what it has passed fades left, what's coming waits right.

  • Zoom: magnification under the lens only; the world keeps its scale everywhere else.
  • Feels like: a tape head reading the path.

+ the "one search moving through a system" story is literal. − the lens limits how much detail a step can show; branches (the bundle) are awkward.

E · Two tracks local and AWS in parallel

Two horizontal lanes, local above and AWS below, with the same stations aligned in columns. The token runs along one lane; a switch drops it to the other at any station.

  • Zoom: a column expands to show both implementations side by side.
  • Best as: the last chapter of B, not the whole film.

+ makes the seam (the one-line swap) visible, which is the architectural point. − it tells the "how it's built" story, not the "what happens in a search" story.

F · Hybrid B/E: the track splits at the seams recommended

One subway line that becomes two tracks (local above, AWS below) exactly where the implementation differs, and merges back where the logic is shared. The active station opens in place across both tracks: what happens in the middle band, how each side does it above and below.

  • Zoom out: every station collapses to a dot; you see one line that splits and rejoins. That shape is the architecture.
  • Zoom in: the station opens across both tracks: the shared "what happens" in the middle band, local above, AWS below.
  • The token: runs on the lit track; the AWS toggle moves it to the other track, and the stats in each opened station change with it.

+ tells both stories at once (what a search does, and where the cloud swap happens) without doubling any content; merged stretches need no second lane. − the opened station is tall (three bands), so the corpus step shows doc cards as a strip, not a grid; the split curves need care at narrow widths.

Recommendation

  • F, the hybrid, for the walkthrough: B's always-visible line, with E's two tracks only where the implementation differs.
  • Where the track splits (from the code): corpus (in memory vs S3), tool call (in-process vs Gateway), retrieval (BM25 vs KB on S3 Vectors), gate (regex vs Guardrails), bundle (disk vs CloudWatch). Where it merges: seed, model view, codename, registry, target, which are the same code on both sides.
  • Keep C only for an MP4 (Claude Motion in claude.ai, or the draft's camera recorded).
